Animate formulas for Manim, glyph by glyph.

The Manim Formula Editor is a video editor for formulas that runs in your browser. Write formulas in LaTeX, place them on a timeline, say where each glyph of one formula goes in the next, and watch the change play. Then export it as a scene for ManimCE or ManimGL, or as a video.

  • Opens from your disk on Linux, macOS and Windows
  • No account, no server
  • No Manim needed to start

01 / GLYPH BY GLYPH

Say where every glyph goes

A transformation is a relation between two formulas: pairs of glyphs, drawn as links from one formula to the next. A glyph can go to one glyph or to several, and several can merge into one. What has no partner leaves with its formula’s outro, or arrives with the intro of the next.

  1. Click a glyph of the upper formula, then one of the lower: that links them.
  2. Delete link removes the links of the selected glyphs; Suggest links by label offers the pairs the editor finds.
  3. Press Preview to play the transformation with your links.

03 / THE CAMERA

Move the camera through the scene

Turn the multicamera on and the frame becomes a camera you move and zoom while the video plays. Here it closes in on c², travels to a formula written outside the first frame, pulls back to show both and returns.

  • Moves are blocks on the timeline’s Camera row, each with its start, duration and easing.
  • Target cameras set a frame aside, for several moves to go to.
  • The video is filmed through the camera, and so is the exported scene: a ManimCE scene becomes a MovingCameraScene, and in both libraries each move is a self.play(…) of the camera’s frame.

05 / EXPORT

Python you can read, for ManimCE or ManimGL

Export ▸ Python writes the scene as a file you can read and change: the same animation for either library, each written its own way.

  • A video

    MP4 or WebM, drawn by the editor and encoded in the browser, with its sound. With the companion, ffmpeg or your own Manim makes it.

    Exporting video

  • A web page

    The scene played live in a page that stops at its pauses: made to be embedded, and extended by hand or by an agent.

    The HTML page

  • Sound and pictures

    The mix of your recordings as WAV or WebM, and the frame at the playhead as a PNG.

06 / SLIDES

Present it, one pause at a time

The Slides mode puts pauses and notes on the scene’s time, and Export ▸ HTML… turns the scene into a page that plays it live and stops at each pause until you go on.

08 / GET IT

Get the editor

Free, and open source under the MIT licence.

  1. 1

    Download it

    The ready-built editor is one ZIP of about 2.5 MB, on the project’s releases page.

    Download 1.0.0
  2. 2

    Open it from your disk

    Unzip it and open its index.html in your browser. A recent Chrome or Edge gives the whole editor; Firefox and Safari save a project by downloading it.

    Browsers
  3. 3

    Add what you need

    The companion, one Python file of the repository, adds exact LaTeX glyphs, font text (on Linux), encoding with ffmpeg and rendering with Manim. Manim itself runs the Python you export.

    The optional companion

Or build it from its source: clone the repository, then npm install and npm run build in app/. Getting the app running
