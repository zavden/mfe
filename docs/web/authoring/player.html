The player

A player draws an example's scene the way the app's Preview draws it: the same evaluator and the same canvas renderer, built into assets/player.js from the app's own code. It needs the example's data script (examples/<slug>.data.js) in the page's head, after player.js. tools/EXAMPLES.md tells how to make an example; this page shows every option of the player with its markup.

The default player

The figure names the example. Press Play, drag the time, step a frame with the arrow keys (Shift: a second), change the speed. Space or K plays and pauses while the player has the focus; a click on the scene does too. 

<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="first-transform"></figure>

With the timeline strip

data-timeline adds a small version of the app's timeline under the scene: its tracks, its clips with their formulas' names, the intro and outro of each clip, the animation blocks and the relations between clips. A press or a drag on it moves the time. 

<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="first-transform" data-timeline
        data-caption="x + 3 = 5 solved in two steps."></figure>

Playing by itself, in a loop

data-autoplay plays while at least half of the player is on screen, and data-loop starts it again at the end. A reader whose system asks for reduced motion is never played to; any player that leaves the screen pauses, and goes on when it comes back. It plays from its data-start (below): give it an instant where the scene shows what it is about, which is what the page shows before it plays and all that reader sees. Here, the last formula, whole. 

<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="hero" data-autoplay data-loop data-start="8" data-timeline></figure>

A part of the scene

data-from and data-to (seconds) play only that part; the time bar and the strip show only it. data-start is the instant shown before Play: a time, or end. Here, the last step of the derivation, shown finished. 

<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="first-transform" data-from="3.8" data-to="6"
        data-start="end" data-timeline></figure>

Fewer controls

data-controls="minimal" keeps Play, the time bar and the time; data-controls="none" keeps the scene alone, which a click (or Space, Enter or K) plays and pauses. data-speed starts it at 0.5, 1 or 2 times its speed. 

<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="hero" data-controls="minimal" data-speed="0.5"></figure>
<figure class="mfe-player" data-mfe-player="first-transform" data-controls="none" data-loop
        data-caption="Click the scene to play it."></figure>

An example the page has not loaded

A figure whose example's script is not in the page shows what is missing, and the console says it too. Nothing else on the page breaks.

Every option

AttributeValueWhat it does
data-mfe-playera slugThe example (required): window.MFE_EXAMPLES[slug], from examples/<slug>.data.js.
data-timelinebooleanThe timeline strip under the scene.
data-autoplaybooleanPlays while at least half of it shows; never with reduced motion.
data-loopbooleanStarts again at the end (the Loop key shows it on).
data-from, data-tosecondsThe part of the scene it plays and shows.
data-startseconds, or endThe instant it shows before it plays (default: the start of its part).
data-controlsfull, minimal, noneIts controls (default full).
data-speed0.5, 1, 2The speed it starts at.
data-captiontextA caption under it, unless the figure has a <figcaption> of its own.

A boolean attribute is on when present (data-loop), off with "false" or "0". A mounted figure carries data-mfe-mounted and says how it stands in data-time, data-progress and data-playing.

The engine, for widgets

MFEPlayer.engine gives a widget the example's project and glyphs, scenes of modified copies of the project, and the drawing. Below, the last step of the hero example drawn alone and large, with the canonical index of each glyph over it — the index a relation pair or a glyph colour names it by. 

const example = MFEPlayer.engine.example('hero');
const scene = example.formulaScene('step4');      // the formula alone, at the centre
MFEPlayer.engine.sizeCanvas(canvas);              // its bitmap on device pixels
const region = MFEPlayer.engine.padBox(scene.contentBox(0), 0.3);
const view = scene.draw(canvas, 0, { region });   // drawn large
for (const [index, box] of scene.geometry(0)[0].glyphs) {
  const { left, top, width } = view.boxToCss(box); // where the glyph is on the canvas
  // … put the index over it
}
