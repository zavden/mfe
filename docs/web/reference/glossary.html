Glossary

The words of the editor and of Manim, each in a sentence or two, with the page that teaches it. A term in italics inside an entry has an entry of its own.

A B C D E F G H I K L M O P R S T U W

A

Anchor
The point of a formula’s box where an end of a line sits: a corner, the middle of an edge or the centre, picked on a compass, or Auto, which lets Manim find where the line leaves the glyphs towards its other end. Lines and arrows
Animation block
See block.
Appear and Disappear
Two kinds of block: an Appear block brings some glyphs in at a time you choose (Write, Create, FadeIn…), a Disappear block takes them out. Animation blocks
Appears with
The field of a formula’s editor that says which intro its new clips get, when it should not be the one chosen in the preferences. Appearing and disappearing
Audio clip
A recording, or a part of one, placed on an audio track, with its own trim and volume curve. Audio
Autosave
The copy of your work that each browser tab keeps by itself, so a reload or a crash does not lose it. It is a safety net: File ▸ Save still writes the real file. Recovering your work

B

Background
The colour of the scene behind everything. A setting of the project. Project settings
Block
A bar in the top half of a clip that animates some of its glyphs at a time: Appear, Disappear, Transform, Morph or Emphasis. Blocks that start together and last the same can form a group. Animation blocks
Brace
An object: a curly brace fitted to the glyphs of another clip, on the side you choose, that can carry a label. It follows what it hangs on. Braces

C

Camera
The frame the scene is filmed through, once the multicamera is on: it can move and zoom while the video plays. In the Preview it is the sky-blue rectangle. The camera
Camera move
A block of the timeline’s Camera row that takes the camera to another frame over a time: a frame of its own, a target camera or the frame of 0 s. Nothing else animates while it runs. The camera
Camera view
The Preview looking through the camera at the playhead, as the video does, instead of showing the world with the camera’s rectangles. The camera
Canonical index
The position of a glyph in the saved list of glyphs of its formula (its snapshot). Every reference in a project to a glyph — a colour, a pair, a block — uses it, so a project does not depend on which renderer drew the formula. Glyph order
Chroma Lab
The colour studio that comes inside the editor: you make a palette there and send it to the editor’s palettes. Palettes and Chroma Lab
Clip
One appearance of an object on the timeline, from a start to an end. A formula can have as many clips as you like, and each clip becomes one Manim object. The timeline
Comment (Slides mode)
Words at an instant of the scene for whoever works on it after the export: a # NOTE in the Python, a note of the exported page and a task of its folder’s guide. Slides and the HTML page
Companion
An optional helper that runs on your own computer, one Python file. With it the editor draws formulas with real LaTeX, shapes font text, encodes video with ffmpeg and can render with Manim. The companion
Compass
The small choice of a side, a corner or the centre that appears when you align by symbol or put the end of a line on a formula. Align by symbol
Copies
One of the three ways (extras) several sources reach one target: the principal source transforms, and the others send a copy and stay where they are. How relations work
Creator
The panel on the left that holds the formulas and texts of the project, the template library and the audio recordings. A tour of the interface
Custom path
The path one pair of glyphs follows, shaped by hand as a spline in the Preview, instead of a straight line or the relation’s path arc. Custom paths

D

Distribute
Spaces three or more selected clips so the gaps between neighbours are equal, horizontally or vertically. Align and distribute
dt
The time between two small ticks of the timeline’s ruler: the zoom’s own, or one of your own set with the − and + beside it. Clips snap to it. The timeline

E

Easing
How the progress of an animation runs over its time: linear, smooth, double_smooth, there_and_back and the others the app offers. Manim calls it a rate function. Appearing and disappearing
Emphasis
A kind of block that draws attention to some glyphs without changing the scene: Indicate, Wiggle, Circumscribe, Flash or FocusOn. Animation blocks
Exact mode
The editor with the companion running and LaTeX installed on its machine: formulas are drawn by real LaTeX, so their glyphs and their order are the ones Manim will build. The companion
Export
Taking the scene out of the editor: as a Python file for ManimCE or ManimGL, as a video, as an audio file or as a web page that plays it. A project file is not an export. Exporting
Extras
The setting of a pair with several sources and one target that says how they reach it: Separate (each source makes its own transform), Merge (Manim) (Manim’s own alignment, where the sources beyond the first fade into the target) or Copies. How relations work

F

Font text
An object that is plain text in any font you choose, with no LaTeX: Text (font) in the Creator. Shaping it needs the companion; until then it is drawn as placeholder boxes, unless the project file already carries its shape. LaTeX texts and font texts
Formula
An object of the Creator whose source is LaTeX in math mode. One formula can have many clips; its name becomes the Python variable of the exported scene. Writing formulas
Frame
The rectangle of the scene that the video shows. In Manim’s units it is 8 tall, and 14.22 wide at 16:9, with its origin at the centre and the y axis pointing up. The Preview

G

Glyph
One drawn piece of a formula: a letter, a digit, an operator, a fraction bar. Colours, pairs, blocks, braces and lines name glyphs. Each has an index and a label. Picking glyphs
Glyph label
What a glyph is, as the editor names it: its character, or a dash for a rule such as a fraction bar. Suggest links by label in the relation editor pairs glyphs that have the same label. The relation editor
Glyph source
The renderer you ask for with the three Glyph source items of the View menu: Automatic, MathJax or LaTeX (companion). Preferences
Group (of blocks)
Blocks of one clip that start together and last the same, drawn as one block until you unfold them. Groups of animation blocks
Group (of formulas)
A named set of formulas that the Creator’s list keeps together, to fold and select as one. Finding and organizing
Guides
The coloured lines the Preview draws while a drag snaps to other formulas, the frame or the grid. The Preview

H

Hidden at the start
A glyph that is off the screen when its clip starts, until an Appear block, a relation or a Morph block brings it in. Animation blocks
Hidden track
A track that the Preview does not draw. It is still exported, and the export dialog says so. The timeline

I

Intro and outro
The animation a clip starts with and the one it ends with, drawn as the hatched ends of the clip on the timeline. The choice None makes the clip appear or disappear at once. Appearing and disappearing

K

Keyframe
The state of a clip — position, scale, rotation, flips, opacity and colour — at a time. A Transform block of the whole formula adds one, and the clip moves to it over the block. Animation blocks

L

LaTeX text
An object that is text with formulas inside it between dollar signs: Text (LaTeX) in the Creator. LaTeX texts and font texts
Line and arrow
An object drawn between two ends: a line, a dashed line, an arrow or a double arrow. Each end is free in the scene or on the glyphs of another clip. Lines and arrows
Link port
The small control in the lower half of a clip that appears while you hold Shift: click it, then another clip, to start a relation. How relations work
Locked region
The time of a camera move, drawn as a hatched band across the tracks: no animation may run in it. The camera
Lock
Three different things. A locked track cannot be edited on the timeline. A clip locked in the Preview is drawn and left alone there, so a click goes through it to what is under it; the timeline and the panels still edit it. And a clip locked after its first transformation (a padlock by its name): the Preview edits its transformations only through the end icon of their blocks. The timeline

M

ManimCE and ManimGL
The two Python libraries the editor writes for: ManimCE, the Community Edition, and ManimGL, 3Blue1Brown’s original. A project has one target, and exports can be made for either. ManimCE and ManimGL
Match colors
A list of LaTeX patterns and the colour each one gives, applied to the glyphs of formulas across the project. Match colors
MathJax
The renderer that is in the browser and needs nothing installed. It draws formulas that look like LaTeX, but the order of its glyphs can differ from LaTeX’s. Glyph order
Morph
A kind of block that moves glyphs of a formula onto other glyphs of the same formula, the way one line of a derivation grows out of the last. Morph blocks
Multicamera
The switch that gives a scene its camera: with it on, the timeline has a Camera row and the Preview shows the world. The camera

O

Offset
Where the window of a relation starts, counted from the start of the target clip. How relations work
Outro
See intro and outro.

P

Package (LaTeX)
A LaTeX package that a formula loads, chosen in its editor under Packages, such as physics or cancel. Writing formulas
Package (.mfe)
A project file that also carries its recordings, so audio travels with it. A project with no audio is usually a plain .mfe.json. Project files
Pair
A link in a relation between glyphs of the source formula and glyphs of the target formula: one to one, one to many or many to one. The pair says which glyph becomes which, and in what style. How relations work
Palette
A set of colours with names, kept in the editor’s palette library. An active palette changes the colours the editor offers and gives new objects. Palettes and Chroma Lab
Pango
The text library Manim uses for font text. The companion shapes text with it on Linux; on other systems font text stays as placeholder boxes. LaTeX texts and font texts
Path arc
The bend of the way glyphs travel in a relation, in degrees: 0 is a straight line. It is Manim’s path_arc. Custom paths
Pause (Slides mode)
An instant where the scene rests and the exported web page stops until its reader goes on; it may carry a note for whoever presents, and skip the wait after it. Slides and the HTML page
Playhead
The vertical line of the timeline that marks the time the Preview shows. Move it by clicking the ruler. The timeline
Positional matching
How an exported scene finds the glyphs of a formula without the companion: by where they are, not by their number, with a small helper at the top of the Python. The generated Python
Preamble
The LaTeX lines added after the packages of every formula, in File ▸ Project settings…. Project settings
Preview
The panel in the middle that draws the scene at the playhead, where you select, move and scale clips. Placing things in the Preview
Project
The document you work on: formulas, tracks, clips, relations and the settings of the scene, saved as one file. Project files

R

Rate function
See easing.
Region
A stretch of time marked on the ruler of the timeline, from a green bracket to a red one: the next animation block, relation or clip of a formula you create takes it, from its start to its end. The timeline
Relation
A transformation of one clip into another: it links a source clip to a target clip through pairs of glyphs and runs during a window of the target clip. Two clips can have several. How relations work
Render with Manim
A way to make a video in Export ▸ Video…: the companion runs the exported Python with your own Manim. It is off unless the companion is started for it. Exporting video
Renderer
What draws the glyphs of a formula: MathJax in the browser, LaTeX through the companion, or Pango for font text. The Renderer badge in the menu bar says which one the project is using. A tour of the interface
Role
A named colour of a palette, such as background or formula: eleven are standard, and you can add roles of your own. Palettes and Chroma Lab

S

Scale step
The step a corner drag of the Preview’s handles snaps its scale to. A preference. Preferences
Shape
An object fitted to a clip or to some of its glyphs: a box, a circle, an underline, a cross, a cross line or a highlight. Shapes
Skip the wait (Slides mode)
What a pause can do: going on from it, the exported web page and the rehearsal jump over the rest that follows, to where the next animation starts. Only where the scene stopped there: played through, in the Python and in a video, the rest stays. Slides and the HTML page
Slides mode
The switch of a project that puts pauses and comments on the scene’s time, for a talk: View ▸ Slides. The ruler then shows where the scene rests. Slides and the HTML page
Snap
The pull that makes a dragged clip or formula stick to the edges, centres and ticks near it. The Snap key of the timeline and the magnet of the Preview turn it on and off. The timeline
Snapshot
The list of a formula’s glyphs that the project saves, whichever renderer drew them. It defines the canonical indices. Project files
Source and target
Two meanings. The source of a formula is the LaTeX you type. In a relation, the source clip is where the glyphs come from and the target clip where they go. How relations work
Split
Dividing a block that names glyphs into several, by runs of consecutive glyphs or as you choose. Timing tools
Standard scale
The size new formulas and texts are born with, where 1 is Manim’s font size 48. A preference. Preferences
Starts empty
A box of a clip’s intro. Ticked, no glyph of the clip comes in with the intro: each appears only when a relation, a Morph block or an Appear block brings it. It makes the target of a transformation show only what was transformed. A clip can also leave only some glyphs out of its intro: the first half of a split clip leaves out those that came on screen after the cut, and its intro says how many, with Bring them back. How relations work, Splitting a clip
Style (of a pair)
How a glyph reaches its target: Transform moves it, Copy (source stays) sends a copy and leaves the source, Fade transform fades one out as the other fades in, Fade copy (source stays) fades a copy and leaves the source. A pair can have its own style or follow its relation’s. How relations work
Suggest
The buttons that propose pairs for you, linking glyphs that have the same label in reading order: Suggest links by label in the relation editor and Suggest pairs by label in the Morph editor. The relation editor, Morph blocks
Sync folder
The folder you pick once with File ▸ Sync folder… to hold your projects, which the Projects dialog then lists. Projects and files

T

Target
The Manim library a project is for, ManimCE or ManimGL: a project setting. In a relation, the target is also the clip the glyphs arrive in. Project settings
Target camera
A frame with a name, drawn in pink in the Preview, set aside for camera moves to go to. Several moves can share one. The camera
Template
A formula written once, with gaps called placeholders (#1, #2…) that you fill in when you use it. The template library
Timeline
The panel at the bottom: tracks of clips under a ruler, with the playhead. It says when each object is on screen. The timeline
Track
A row of the timeline that holds clips. Visual tracks can be hidden or locked, and the first one is drawn in front; audio tracks can be muted and locked. The timeline
Transform
A kind of block that moves, rotates, rescales or recolours a whole object or some of its glyphs. Not to be confused with a relation, which turns one clip into another. Animation blocks

U

Unlink formula
Giving a clip a copy of its formula of its own, so that editing one no longer changes the other. Timing tools
Unpaired glyphs
Glyphs of the source that no pair takes, which leave with the source clip’s outro, and glyphs of the target that no pair brings, which arrive with the target clip’s intro, or stay out when the target starts empty. A glyph that a block of its clip takes away or brings in is the block’s. How relations work

W

World (view)
What the Preview shows while the multicamera is on: the scene around the frame, nothing cut, with the camera and the target cameras drawn as rectangles. The camera
Window (of a relation)
The stretch of time of the target clip during which a relation’s transformation runs, set by its offset and its duration. On the timeline it is a band you can drag and resize. How relations work
